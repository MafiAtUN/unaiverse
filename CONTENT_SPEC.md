| 4 | 💻 **Data & Digital** | OICT, data cells, UN 2.0, statisticians | What's technically real vs declaratory; standards, interoperability, internal adoption |
| 5 | 🎩 **Front Office** | Chiefs of staff, speechwriters, briefing-note writers | The 30-second brief, the safe citable line, what's landing on the principal's desk next |
| 6 | 🏛️ **OPGA** | The President of the General Assembly's team | What sits under **GA authority** vs the SG's; what the PGA must convene, appoint, or steer; member-state dynamics (consensus vs vote); what strengthens the GA's centrality; usable lines for PGA statements |
| 7 | 🛠️ **Builders** | AI practitioners in/around the UN system who want to *build* | What's actually usable: datasets, standards, guidance-as-compliance-checklist, sandboxes, funding channels, pilots to learn from; what to align with so a prototype survives review |
| 8 | 🗺️ **Permanent Missions** | Member State delegations in NY & Geneva, capital desk officers | Negotiation posture and history (consensus vs vote, who led what); which processes need delegate coverage and when; what to report to capital; where influence is still up for grabs |

**Persona-writing notes for the two newest:**
- **OPGA** is an *institutional* lens, not a personal one. Its takes track the GA-vs-Secretariat center of gravity: resolutions, mandated processes, review cycles, appointments, and modalities are its native language. Where other personas ask "what does this mean for my work," OPGA asks "what does this obligate or empower the Assembly — and its President — to do, and by when."
- **Builders** is the most concrete lens on the site. Zero ceremony: if an event produced nothing a builder can use, comply with, or learn from, the take says so honestly ("nothing to build with here — but it shapes the rules you'll build under").
- **Permanent Missions** is capital-facing where OPGA is Assembly-facing. Its takes answer the delegate's three eternal questions: *do I need to cover this, what do I tell my capital, and is there still room to shape it?* Especially mind small missions — for a three-diplomat delegation drowning in processes, "this is the one AI meeting worth staffing" is the most valuable sentence on the site. **Absolute neutrality rule:** never characterize any State's or group's position as good, bad, winning, or losing — describe processes and opportunities, not sides.

## 4. The three-beat formula (every take, no exceptions)

~60 words total, three labeled beats:

- **So what:** one sentence — what this changes for this reader's work
- **Watch for:** the concrete next thing to track (mandated report, review date, negotiation, deadline)
- **Use it:** how to deploy it — citable line, document symbol, meeting to attend, checklist to apply

**Hard rules:** No invented facts — every claim traceable to the milestone's research.md entry or its linked sources. No hedging filler. Tone follows §4 of the master plan (wit welcome, nothing that embarrasses the UN or any Member State). Second person ("you"), active voice.

## 5. Worked examples

**A/RES/79/325 (Scientific Panel + Global Dialogue) — OPGA take**
> **So what:** The GA — not the Secretariat — owns both flagship AI mechanisms: the Panel reports through GA processes and the Dialogue's modalities, co-chairs, and calendar run on Assembly decisions.
> **Watch for:** The May 2027 Dialogue in New York — co-chair selection and modalities land squarely in PGA territory.
> **Use it:** The strongest single proof point that the Assembly is the center of gravity in global AI governance; safe in any PGA statement.

**CEB Principles for Ethical Use of AI (2022) — Builders take**
> **So what:** This is the de facto compliance baseline for anything you build inside the UN system — align early or stall at review.
> **Watch for:** Entity-level implementations and Secretariat guidance on generative AI, which turn principles into checklists.
> **Use it:** Map your concept note to the principles explicitly (symbol: CEB/2022/2/Add.1) — reviewers look for it.

**A/RES/79/325 (Scientific Panel + Global Dialogue) — Permanent Missions take**
> **So what:** Two new standing processes your delegation now needs to cover — the Panel's reporting cycle and the recurring Global Dialogue — with working methods still taking shape.
> **Watch for:** The May 2027 Dialogue in New York: modalities and statement slots reward early engagement.
> **Use it:** If you can only staff one AI process, this is it — and the Panel's reports are free, citable technical backing for national statements.

**Global Digital Compact — Front Office take**
> **So what:** First universal AI-governance commitments your principal can cite without caveats — adopted by consensus, all 193.
> **Watch for:** The high-level GDC review at the GA's 82nd session; implementation language is being negotiated now.
> **Use it:** Cite as A/RES/79/1, Annex I. Safe line: "the first truly universal agreement on AI governance."

## 7. Azure generation prompt (per milestone, per persona)

Paste as the system message; send one milestone × persona per call, temperature low.

```
You write "takes" for an interactive website about the UN's work
on AI. A take tells one specific professional audience what a
milestone means for them.

AUDIENCE FOR THIS TAKE: {persona name + full row from §3}

FORMAT (exactly three labeled beats, ≤70 words total):
So what: <one sentence — what this changes for this reader>
Watch for: <the concrete next thing to track: date, report, process>
Use it: <how to deploy it: citable symbol, safe quotable line,
checklist, meeting, or dataset>

RULES:
- Use ONLY facts in the milestone entry below. Do not add events,
  dates, numbers, or claims from your own knowledge. If the entry
  lacks something a beat needs, write the beat without it.
- Second person, active voice, plain verbs. No filler, no hedging.
- Wit is welcome; sarcasm only at the expense of hype or
  bureaucratic pace in general — NEVER at the UN, its officials,
  or any Member State. Nothing screenshot-riskable.
- If the entry is marked [UNVERIFIED], do not state the unverified
  detail as fact.

MILESTONE ENTRY:
{paste the full research.md entry, including sources}
```

**Pipeline order:** generate → Mafi's review pass (voice + §4 tone test) → Claude Code ingests as JSON. Budget review time: 135 takes ≈ one focused evening. Prompt GPT in batches per persona (all OPGA takes together, etc.) — persona voice stays consistent that way.

## 8. QA checklist before any take ships
- [ ] Three beats, ≤70 words, correct labels
- [ ] Every factual claim traceable to research.md or its sources
- [ ] Passes the §4 town-hall test
- [ ] "Use it" beat contains something genuinely actionable (symbol, date, line, or link) — not vibes
- [ ] Persona voice check: would this reader feel it was written *for them*?
